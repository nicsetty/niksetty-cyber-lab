Connecting to TryHackMe with OpenVPN

Understanding what a VPN connection actually does, how to verify it, and what to troubleshoot when the login prompt appears.

01 / The idea

TryHackMe hosts target machines on a private lab network. OpenVPN can create an encrypted tunnel from your own computer to that network, allowing locally installed tools to reach authorized lab targets.

02 / What you need

  • A TryHackMe account and your own downloaded .ovpn profile.
  • An OpenVPN-compatible client on your computer.
  • An active TryHackMe room with a reachable lab target.

03 / Connect

Download your profile from TryHackMe Access, import it into your OpenVPN-compatible client and connect. On macOS, an alternative is the OpenVPN command-line client if installed:

sudo openvpn path/to/your-profile.ovpn

Use the actual filename on your computer. The sudo prompt, when present, is for your local Mac password; VPN authentication is determined by your profile and service configuration.

04 / Verify the tunnel

Check for a tunnel interface and relevant routes:

ifconfig | grep -A 5 utun
netstat -rn

OpenVPN output often shows Initialization Sequence Completed after successful connection. A room target can then be tested, as permitted by the room:

ping <YOUR_LAB_TARGET_IP>

Some targets block ICMP, so a failed ping alone does not prove the VPN is disconnected.

05 / Common issues

  • Credentials prompt: Ensure you imported the correct original profile and follow the client’s authentication instructions. Do not paste VPN credentials into public posts.
  • Cannot reach the target: Confirm the room target is started, the VPN is connected, and routes are set.
  • Wrong region/profile: Download a fresh configuration for your selected server.

06 / Key takeaway

OpenVPN is connectivity, not a hacking tool. Your commands and security tools run locally; the tunnel transports relevant traffic to the training network.

Publication note: This article is an educational guide, not a claim that any specific TryHackMe room was completed. Keep room flags, sensitive details and private configurations out of public repositories.
